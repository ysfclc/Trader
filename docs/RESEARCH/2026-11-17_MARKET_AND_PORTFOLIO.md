# Initial Market and Portfolio Assessment

Date: 2026-11-17

This is a starting hypothesis and reuse map, not proof of current demand or a claim that prospects have been contacted. Revalidate repository contents and public market facts before acting on them.

## Initial commercial wedge

**Türkiye ↔ Poland/EU industrial tooling and machining.**

Why start here:
- The operator already has a substantial Türkiye network that includes manufacturers and suppliers.
- The operator has first-hand manufacturing experience: CNC machining, injection-mould tooling, machine programming and design, with practical familiarity around production tolerances.
- This gives a better basis for asking technical qualification questions than choosing an arbitrary trending product.
- Poland provides a practical first buyer market and a base for EU-facing conversations.

Do not assume that every Turkish contact is export-ready or that every Polish company is a buyer. Verify each company's actual capability, demand, authority, quality systems, capacity, export history and willingness to communicate.

## First categories to test

Treat these as discovery hypotheses, not locked product taxonomy:
- CNC machining and precision-turned/milled components.
- Injection moulds, tooling, mould components and maintenance.
- Sheet-metal fabrication and industrial parts.
- Custom fixtures, jigs and production aids.
- Small-batch or overflow manufacturing capacity.

Start with made-to-spec B2B work rather than speculative commodity inventory. A buyer requirement with drawings/specifications and delivery expectations is more actionable than a generic statement such as "looking for suppliers."

## Qualification checklist

### Buyer
- Exact part/product and application.
- Drawing, material, dimensions, tolerances and finish.
- Quantity, annual volume and repeat potential.
- Target price or acceptable price basis.
- Delivery location, Incoterms preference and deadline.
- Required certificates, inspection and traceability.
- Current pain: price, lead time, quality, capacity or second source.
- Decision-maker and purchasing process.
- Evidence that the requirement is current and the person is authorized to discuss it.

### Supplier
- Legal company identity and production location.
- Actual machines/processes, dimensions/capacity and materials.
- Quality certifications and inspection equipment.
- Relevant sample work and references.
- MOQ, capacity, lead time and quote validity.
- Export experience, packaging and logistics capability.
- Ability to review drawings under an appropriate confidentiality process.
- Named contact and permission to introduce the company.

## Commercial model to test

Begin as a transparent introducer, not an undisclosed reseller:
1. Confirm the buyer's need and permission to source.
2. Confirm supplier capability and permission to introduce.
3. Agree in writing who pays any introduction/commission fee, what triggers it, how long protection lasts and what happens on repeat orders.
4. Disclose the intermediary role where required.
5. Avoid promising quality, delivery, certification or exclusivity that has not been independently verified.

Before taking commission or handling goods, validate applicable Polish/EU and Turkish tax, customs, VAT, product compliance, agency and contract requirements with qualified professionals. Do not collect customer funds or represent either side as an agent without a clear agreement.

## Prospect discovery plan

Find demand signals first:
- Polish manufacturers publicly seeking machining, tooling, components, second sources or shorter lead times.
- Procurement and supplier-quality roles at relevant manufacturers.
- Turkish suppliers with documented processes and export capability.
- Trade associations, industrial clusters, exhibitor lists and public RFQ/procurement notices.

For each lead, record the exact public source, date, need/capability evidence, relevant contact role, fit rationale and next verification question. Do not treat a company directory listing as proof of buying intent.

## First outreach principle

No generic "we connect buyers and suppliers" blast. Use one specific, sourced reason for contacting each side. Ask a low-friction qualification question. Do not disclose buyer drawings or supplier confidential information without authorization.

## Success metrics

- Verified current buyer needs.
- Verified supplier capabilities.
- Buyer needs with at least one technically plausible supplier.
- Supplier willingness to quote.
- Permission-based introductions.
- Replies, RFQs, quotes, sample orders and paid outcomes.

Raw scraped profile count is not a success metric.
